What Weight conversions measure

Weight conversions cover units such as kilograms, pounds, and grams. Converting between them lets you move between the different measurement systems and conventions used across regions and industries.

Common real-world use

Common real-world uses include commerce and retail pricing, medicine and dosing, agriculture and livestock, and shipping and logistics.

Choosing a unit

Pick the unit your source data, audience, or regional convention already uses, and convert once at the start of a calculation rather than mixing systems partway through.

About Weight Conversion

Weight and mass conversions cover how we measure everything from body weight to shipping cargo, and the terms are often used interchangeably in everyday language even though physicists distinguish mass (an intrinsic property of matter) from weight (the force gravity exerts on that mass). For practical conversion purposes, this category treats them the way most people do: kilograms, grams, pounds, and ounces used to describe how heavy something is. The kilogram anchors the metric side as the SI base unit, while the pound anchors the US customary and imperial side.

The pound used in the US, UK, and most English-speaking countries today is the international avoirdupois pound, standardized in 1959 as exactly 0.45359237 kilograms, which finally aligned historically inconsistent national pound definitions. Common weight units span a huge range, from milligrams used in pharmacy dosing to metric tons and US short tons used in shipping and industry. This converter supports everyday units like kilograms, grams, pounds, and ounces, along with larger units for freight and smaller units for precision measurement, all built on the exact international pound and kilogram definitions.

Weight conversion accuracy carries different stakes depending on context: a shipping company calculating freight costs, a nutritionist tracking food intake in grams, and someone converting their body weight between kilograms and pounds all rely on the same underlying pound-to-kilogram relationship, just applied to very different scales and consequences. Because the pound and kilogram are both in such widespread daily use, from bathroom scales to grocery store price tags to airline baggage limits, having a reliable, precise weight converter is one of the most practically useful tools on a unit conversion site.

It's worth remembering that 'weight' in everyday conversion contexts (kilograms, pounds) refers to mass, not the scientific definition of weight as a gravitational force, so these conversions apply consistently regardless of location on Earth. The distinction only becomes practically relevant in specialized contexts like spaceflight or physics, where an object's mass stays constant while its true weight (the force of gravity acting on it) would change in a different gravitational environment.

Frequently asked questions

Is weight the same thing as mass?

Not technically. Mass is the amount of matter in an object and doesn't change with location, while weight is the force gravity exerts on that mass and does vary, for instance an object weighs less on the Moon. In everyday conversions, kilograms and pounds are used as if they measure the same thing, which is accurate enough on Earth's surface.

How many grams are in a pound?

One pound equals exactly 453.59237 grams, based on the international avoirdupois pound standardized in 1959.

What is the difference between a US ton and a metric ton?

A US short ton is 2,000 pounds (about 907.18 kg), a UK long ton is 2,240 pounds (about 1,016.05 kg), and a metric ton (tonne) is exactly 1,000 kg (about 2,204.6 lb). All three are called 'tons' but differ meaningfully.

How do I convert pounds and ounces to just pounds?

Divide the ounces by 16 and add the result to the whole pounds, since there are 16 ounces in a pound. For example, 8 lb 4 oz is 8 + (4 ÷ 16) = 8.25 lb.

Why is a stone equal to 14 pounds?

The stone is a traditional British unit historically used for weighing wool and other goods, and it was standardized at 14 pounds in the 19th century. It remains common in the UK and Ireland for measuring body weight.

What's the most precise unit for a kitchen scale?

Grams are typically the most practical precise unit for kitchen use, since they allow fine measurement of small quantities like spices or yeast without the awkward fractions that ounces or pounds would require.

When Should I Use Each Weight Unit?

  • Kilogram — Used internationally as the base unit for body weight, groceries, and shipping.
  • Pound — Used in the US and UK for body weight and everyday product labeling.
  • Ounce — Used in the US for small package and food product weights.
  • Metric ton — Used internationally for bulk cargo, industrial materials, and commodity trading.

Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Mixing metric tons with US short tons, which differ by about 10%.
  • ❌ Confusing weight (a force) with mass, though in everyday conversion they're treated the same on Earth.
  • ❌ Using fluid ounces (a volume unit) interchangeably with weight ounces, which measure different things.
  • ❌ Rounding shipping weight conversions without accounting for how carriers price by weight brackets.

History

The kilogram is the only SI base unit still named with a prefix, a historical quirk dating back to the 1795 metric reforms, when it was defined as the mass of a liter of water. For over a century, a physical platinum-iridium cylinder in France served as the literal reference kilogram until 2019, when scientists redefined it in terms of a fundamental physical constant, removing the need for a single physical object anywhere in the world. The pound has far older and messier roots, descending from the Roman "libra," which is why pound is still abbreviated "lb" today. The pound was used in various regional definitions across medieval Europe before England standardized it, and the US inherited that pre-metric English pound even as most of the world eventually moved to the kilogram, leaving weight as one of the most commonly converted measurement categories in global trade today.
