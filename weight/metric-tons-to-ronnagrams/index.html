Understanding the Metric ton

Metric ton (t) measures mass. A tonne is exactly 1,000 kilograms. It is classified as a Non-SI unit accepted for use with SI (also called metric ton).

Where it's used: Used worldwide except the US, which uses the short ton (2,000 lb)

Tonne vs. US (short) ton vs. UK (long) ton. The metric tonne (1,000 kg ≈ 2,204.6 lb) is different from both the US short ton (2,000 lb ≈ 907.2 kg) and the UK long ton (2,240 lb ≈ 1,016 kg) — all three are used in different shipping and industrial contexts and should not be assumed interchangeable.

Uses of the Metric ton today

  • International shipping and freight weight
  • Industrial and commodity trade (steel, grain, shipping) worldwide
  • Vehicle and cargo weight ratings outside the US

History of the Metric ton

The tonne follows directly from the kilogram via the standard 'kilo-' scaling by 1,000, adopted as a convenient large-mass unit alongside the metric system's spread through international trade and industry from the 19th century onward.

Historical origin. Metric ton was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
