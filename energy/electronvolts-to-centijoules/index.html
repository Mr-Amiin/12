About Converting Electronvolts to Centijoules

Electronvolt and Centijoule both measure energy, and the conversion is especially useful for comparing nutrition labels (kilocalories) against scientific energy figures (joules). Electronvolt is most often used for particle physics (particle accelerator energies are often quoted in gev or tev).

Formula

centijoules = electronvolts × 1.602177e-17

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 electronvolt equals 1.602177e-19 base units, and 1 centijoule equals 0.01 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct electronvolt-to-centijoule factor of 1.602177e-17.

Understanding the Electronvolt

Electronvolt (eV) measures energy. The energy gained by a single electron accelerated through an electric potential difference of one volt — a very small amount of energy, exactly 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ joules. It is classified as a non-SI unit accepted for use with the SI, common in physics.

Where it's used: Standard unit throughout particle physics, atomic physics, and chemistry worldwide

Uses of the Electronvolt today

  • Particle physics (particle accelerator energies are often quoted in GeV or TeV)
  • Atomic and molecular energy levels
  • Semiconductor band-gap energies
