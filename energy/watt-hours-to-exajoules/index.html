About Converting Watt-hours to Exajoules

Watt-hour and Exajoule both measure energy, and the conversion is especially useful for comparing nutrition labels (kilocalories) against scientific energy figures (joules). Watt-hour is most often used for phone, laptop, and power-bank battery capacity ratings.

Formula

exajoules = watt-hours × 3.6e-15

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 watt-hour equals 3600 base units, and 1 exajoule equals 1e+18 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct watt-hour-to-exajoule factor of 3.6e-15.

Simple example

1 Wh × 3.6e-15 = 3.6e-15 EJ

1 watt-hour = 3.6e-15 exajoules.

Real-world example

100 Wh × 3.6e-15 = 3.6e-13 EJ

100 watt-hours = 3.6e-13 exajoules.

Understanding the Watt-hour

Watt-hour (Wh) measures energy. The energy delivered by one watt of power sustained for one hour — equal to 3,600 joules. It is classified as a metric derived energy unit.

Where it's used: Universal — standard consumer-electronics and battery energy unit worldwide

Wh vs. kWh. The same unit at different scales: 1,000 Wh equals 1 kWh. Wh suits small consumer-electronics batteries; kWh is used for household electricity billing and larger energy storage.

Uses of the Watt-hour today

  • Phone, laptop, and power-bank battery capacity ratings
  • Electric-vehicle battery capacity specifications
  • Small-scale renewable energy (solar panel) output ratings
