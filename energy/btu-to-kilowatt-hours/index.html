About Converting BTU to kWhs

BTU and kWh both measure energy, and the conversion is especially useful for comparing nutrition labels (kilocalories) against scientific energy figures (joules). BTU is typically associated with united States (HVAC, heating, and appliance energy ratings), while kWh is typically associated with used worldwide for electricity billing.

Understanding the BTU

BTU (BTU) measures energy. A British thermal unit is defined as exactly 1,055.05585262 joules (the International Table BTU). It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (HVAC, heating, and appliance energy ratings)

Uses of the BTU today

  • Air conditioner and furnace capacity ratings in the US
  • Water heater and appliance energy specifications
  • US energy industry (natural gas is often billed via BTU content)

History of the BTU

The BTU originated in 19th-century steam engineering as the heat needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit, paralleling the calorie's metric definition. Several slightly different BTU values existed historically before the 'International Table' BTU was standardized at exactly 1,055.05585262 joules.

Historical origin. BTU was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the kWh

kWh (kWh) measures energy. A kilowatt-hour is the energy of one kilowatt of power sustained for one hour: exactly 3,600,000 joules. It is classified as a Non-SI unit accepted for use with SI.

Where it's used: Used worldwide for electricity billing

Uses of the kWh today

  • Residential and commercial electricity billing worldwide
  • Battery capacity ratings (especially electric vehicles)
  • Energy policy and grid-scale power statistics

History of the kWh

The kilowatt-hour became the standard electricity billing unit as power utilities expanded in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, since it conveniently matches the scale of typical household electricity consumption, unlike the much smaller joule.

Historical origin. kWh was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
