Understanding the Revolutions per minute

Revolutions per minute (rpm) measures rotational speed. RPM (revolutions per minute) equals hertz divided by 60, since one revolution per minute is 1/60 of a cycle per second. It is classified as a Non-SI unit (mechanical/automotive).

Where it's used: Used worldwide in automotive, mechanical, and industrial contexts

Uses of the Revolutions per minute today

  • Engine and motor speed specifications (car tachometers)
  • Industrial machinery and turbine speed ratings
  • Hard drive and disc rotational speed

History of the Revolutions per minute

RPM developed alongside rotating machinery in the 19th-century industrial era as a practical, directly-readable way to describe rotational speed (a tachometer counting revolutions per minute), rather than converting to the more abstract cycles-per-second used in physics.

Historical origin. Revolutions per minute was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Decahertz (daHz)
