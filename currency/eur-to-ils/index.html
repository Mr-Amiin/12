Understanding the Euro

Euro (€) measures currency. EUR is the official currency of the eurozone; its value against other currencies floats on foreign exchange markets rather than being fixed. It is classified as a ISO 4217 currency code.

Where it's used: 20 European Union member states forming the eurozone, plus several microstates and territories that use it by agreement

Uses of the Euro today

  • Domestic transactions across the eurozone
  • International trade invoicing within and with the EU
  • Second-most-held reserve currency globally after the US dollar

History of the Euro

The euro was introduced as an accounting currency in 1999 and entered physical circulation as banknotes and coins on 1 January 2002, replacing the national currencies (such as the Deutsche Mark and French franc) of the founding eurozone member states.

Its creation followed the Maastricht Treaty (1992) and was managed by the European Central Bank, established in 1998 specifically to oversee the new currency and eurozone monetary policy.

Historical origin. Euro was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
