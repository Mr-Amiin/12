Understanding the Pound Sterling

Pound Sterling (£) measures currency. GBP is the official currency of the United Kingdom; its value floats on foreign exchange markets. It is classified as a ISO 4217 currency code.

Where it's used: United Kingdom, plus several British Crown Dependencies and Overseas Territories

Uses of the Pound Sterling today

  • Domestic UK transactions
  • International trade and finance (London is a major FX trading hub)
  • Reserve currency holdings

History of the Pound Sterling

The pound sterling traces its name and unit to Anglo-Saxon England (a pound weight of sterling silver), making it one of the oldest currencies still in use, though its modern decimal form (100 pence to the pound) dates only to 1971's decimalization.

Historical origin. Pound Sterling was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Pound Sterling — sources
