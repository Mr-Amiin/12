Understanding the Chinese Yuan

Chinese Yuan (¥) measures currency. CNY (renminbi, unit yuan) is the official currency of China; its exchange rate is managed within a band by the People's Bank of China rather than freely floating. It is classified as a ISO 4217 currency code.

Where it's used: Mainland China

Managed vs. floating exchange rate. Unlike USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY, the yuan's exchange rate is managed within a daily trading band set by China's central bank, not fully market-determined.

Uses of the Chinese Yuan today

  • Domestic Chinese transactions
  • International trade with China
  • Growing use in international reserves and trade settlement

History of the Chinese Yuan

The renminbi was introduced in 1948 by the People's Bank of China, replacing the currencies used by different regions during the civil war period, and became the sole legal currency of mainland China after 1949.

Historical origin. Chinese Yuan was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Mexican Peso

Mexican Peso ($) measures currency. MXN is the official currency of Mexico; its value floats on foreign exchange markets. It is classified as a ISO 4217 currency code.

Where it's used: Mexico

Uses of the Mexican Peso today

  • Domestic Mexican transactions
  • Major US-Mexico trade and remittance corridor
  • Tourism

History of the Mexican Peso

The modern Mexican peso was introduced in 1993 ('nuevo peso') at a rate of 1,000 old pesos, a revaluation addressing decades of inflation, before dropping the 'nuevo' label in 1996.

Historical origin. Mexican Peso was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
