About Converting GB to Gigabits

GB and Gigabit are everyday digital-storage units, used for everything from file sizes and app downloads to storage device capacity and mobile data allowances. GB is typically associated with used worldwide as a standard digital storage unit, while Gigabit is typically associated with used worldwide for high-speed network ratings (fiber internet, data centers).

Formula

gigabits = gb × 8

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 gb equals 8000000000 base units, and 1 gigabit equals 1000000000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct gb-to-gigabit factor of 8.

Understanding the GB

GB (GB) measures digital storage. In the decimal convention, 1 gigabyte equals 1,000 megabytes (10⁹ bytes). It is classified as a Decimal (SI-prefixed) digital storage unit.

Where it's used: Used worldwide as a standard digital storage unit

Uses of the GB today

  • Storage device and SSD/HDD capacity labeling
  • Mobile data plan allowances
  • Software and media file size reporting

History of the GB

Like the megabyte, the gigabyte inherited ambiguity between decimal (10⁹) and binary (2³⁰) interpretations from early computing conventions, resolved formally by the 1998 IEC binary-prefix standard, which reserves 'gigabyte' for the decimal meaning and 'gibibyte' (GiB) for the binary one.

Historical origin. GB was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Gigabit

Gigabit (Gb) measures digital storage/transfer. A gigabit is 1,000 megabits (125 megabytes) under the decimal convention used for network speeds. It is classified as a Decimal (SI-prefixed) digital unit.

Where it's used: Used worldwide for high-speed network ratings (fiber internet, data centers)

Uses of the Gigabit today

  • Gigabit fiber internet service advertising
  • Data center and enterprise network bandwidth

History of the Gigabit

As internet speeds crossed the 1,000 Mbps threshold with fiber-optic and enterprise networking, 'gigabit' became the standard marketing term for these connections, following the same bits-per-second convention established for slower network speeds.

Historical origin. Gigabit was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
