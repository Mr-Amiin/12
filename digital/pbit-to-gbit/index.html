About Converting Petabits to Gigabits

Petabit and Gigabit are everyday digital-storage units, used for everything from file sizes and app downloads to storage device capacity and mobile data allowances. Specifically: Petabit digital storage unit, and gigabit digital storage unit.

Formula

gigabits = petabits × 1000000

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petabit equals 1e+15 base units, and 1 gigabit equals 1000000000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petabit-to-gigabit factor of 1000000.

Simple example

1 Pb × 1000000 = 1,000,000 Gb

1 petabit = 1,000,000 gigabits.

Real-world example

500 Pb × 1000000 = 500,000,000 Gb

500 petabits = 500,000,000 gigabits.

Understanding the Gigabit

Gigabit (Gb) measures digital storage/transfer. A gigabit is 1,000 megabits (125 megabytes) under the decimal convention used for network speeds. It is classified as a Decimal (SI-prefixed) digital unit.

Where it's used: Used worldwide for high-speed network ratings (fiber internet, data centers)

Uses of the Gigabit today

  • Gigabit fiber internet service advertising
  • Data center and enterprise network bandwidth

History of the Gigabit

As internet speeds crossed the 1,000 Mbps threshold with fiber-optic and enterprise networking, 'gigabit' became the standard marketing term for these connections, following the same bits-per-second convention established for slower network speeds.

Historical origin. Gigabit was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
