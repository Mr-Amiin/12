About Converting Knots to Centimeter per Minutes

Knots and Centimeter per Minutes both describe speed, and the conversion matters whenever a maritime or aviation speed (knots) needs to be compared against a road speed (mph). Knots is most often used for ship and boat speed in marine navigation.

Understanding the Knots

Knots (kn) measures speed. A knot is one nautical mile per hour; since the nautical mile is exactly 1,852 meters, a knot is exactly 1,852/3,600 m/s. It is classified as a Non-SI unit accepted for use with SI (maritime/aviation).

Where it's used: Used internationally in maritime navigation and aviation

Uses of the Knots today

  • Ship and boat speed in marine navigation
  • Aircraft airspeed and wind speed reporting in aviation
  • Marine weather forecasts (wind speed)

History of the Knots

The knot gets its name from an old nautical method of measuring speed: sailors trailed a 'log line' with evenly spaced knots behind a ship and counted how many knots paid out over a fixed time, giving a direct speed reading in 'knots.'

The nautical mile it's based on was itself standardized internationally in 1929 (the International Nautical Mile, 1,852 m exactly), tying the knot to a precise value still used in shipping and aviation today.

Historical origin. Knots was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
