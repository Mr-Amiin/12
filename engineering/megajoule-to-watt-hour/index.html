About Converting Megajoules to Watt-hours

Megajoule and Watt-hour both measure a quantity used in structural or mechanical engineering — commonly stress, pressure, force, torque, power, or energy — where converting between the unit conventions used in different regions, industries, or equipment specifications is a routine part of design work. Specifically: Megajoule is 1000000 joule units, and a watt-hour is 3,600 joules.

Formula

watt-hours = megajoules × 277.777777778

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 megajoule equals 1000000 base units, and 1 watt-hour equals 3600 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct megajoule-to-watt-hour factor of 277.777777778.

Understanding the Watt-hour

Watt-hour (Wh) measures energy. The energy delivered by one watt of power sustained for one hour — equal to 3,600 joules. It is classified as a metric derived energy unit.

Where it's used: Universal — standard consumer-electronics and battery energy unit worldwide

Wh vs. kWh. The same unit at different scales: 1,000 Wh equals 1 kWh. Wh suits small consumer-electronics batteries; kWh is used for household electricity billing and larger energy storage.

Uses of the Watt-hour today

  • Phone, laptop, and power-bank battery capacity ratings
  • Electric-vehicle battery capacity specifications
  • Small-scale renewable energy (solar panel) output ratings
