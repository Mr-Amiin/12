Understanding the Newton meter

Newton meter (N m) measures torque. The newton-meter is the SI derived unit of torque: one newton of force applied at one meter of leverage. It is classified as a SI derived unit.

Where it's used: Standard scientific and automotive torque unit worldwide except the US

Uses of the Newton meter today

  • Torque wrench specifications (bolt tightening) outside the US
  • Engine torque specifications internationally
  • Mechanical and structural engineering

History of the Newton meter

As a coherent SI derived unit, the newton-meter follows automatically from the newton and meter, without an independent historical origin — though it is numerically identical in form to the joule (energy), the two are conventionally kept distinct because torque and energy are physically different quantities.

Historical origin. Newton meter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Why do engineering unit conventions vary so much by region and industry?

Pressure, force, and stress can be expressed in metric (pascal, newton), US customary (psi, pound-force), or older and industry-specific units (atmosphere, torr, kip) depending on the country, industry, and even the equipment manufacturer, which is why engineers routinely need to convert between them when working across specs from different sources.
