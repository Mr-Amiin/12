Understanding the Terajoule (TJ)

Understanding the kWh

kWh (kWh) measures energy. A kilowatt-hour is the energy of one kilowatt of power sustained for one hour: exactly 3,600,000 joules. It is classified as a Non-SI unit accepted for use with SI.

Where it's used: Used worldwide for electricity billing

Uses of the kWh today

  • Residential and commercial electricity billing worldwide
  • Battery capacity ratings (especially electric vehicles)
  • Energy policy and grid-scale power statistics

History of the kWh

The kilowatt-hour became the standard electricity billing unit as power utilities expanded in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, since it conveniently matches the scale of typical household electricity consumption, unlike the much smaller joule.

Historical origin. kWh was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Why do engineering unit conventions vary so much by region and industry?

Pressure, force, and stress can be expressed in metric (pascal, newton), US customary (psi, pound-force), or older and industry-specific units (atmosphere, torr, kip) depending on the country, industry, and even the equipment manufacturer, which is why engineers routinely need to convert between them when working across specs from different sources.
