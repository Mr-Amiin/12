About Converting Watt-hours to Attojoules

Watt-hour and Attojoule both measure a quantity used in structural or mechanical engineering — commonly stress, pressure, force, torque, power, or energy — where converting between the unit conventions used in different regions, industries, or equipment specifications is a routine part of design work. Watt-hour is most often used for phone, laptop, and power-bank battery capacity ratings.

Formula

attojoules = watt-hours × 3.6e+21

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 watt-hour equals 3600 base units, and 1 attojoule equals 1e-18 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct watt-hour-to-attojoule factor of 3.6e+21.

Understanding the Watt-hour

Watt-hour (Wh) measures energy. The energy delivered by one watt of power sustained for one hour — equal to 3,600 joules. It is classified as a metric derived energy unit.

Where it's used: Universal — standard consumer-electronics and battery energy unit worldwide

Wh vs. kWh. The same unit at different scales: 1,000 Wh equals 1 kWh. Wh suits small consumer-electronics batteries; kWh is used for household electricity billing and larger energy storage.

Uses of the Watt-hour today

  • Phone, laptop, and power-bank battery capacity ratings
  • Electric-vehicle battery capacity specifications
  • Small-scale renewable energy (solar panel) output ratings
