About Converting Picowatts to Milliwatts

Picowatt and Milliwatt both measure a quantity used in structural or mechanical engineering — commonly stress, pressure, force, torque, power, or energy — where converting between the unit conventions used in different regions, industries, or equipment specifications is a routine part of design work. Specifically: Picowatt is 1e-12 watt units, and milliwatt is 0.001 watt units.

Formula

milliwatts = picowatts × 1e-9

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 picowatt equals 1e-12 base units, and 1 milliwatt equals 0.001 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct picowatt-to-milliwatt factor of 1e-9.

Simple example

1 pW × 1e-9 = 1e-9 mW

1 picowatt = 1e-9 milliwatts.

Real-world example

100 pW × 1e-9 = 1e-7 mW

100 picowatts = 1e-7 milliwatts.

How many milliwatts are in 1 picowatt?

1 picowatt equals 1e-9 milliwatts, using the exact defined conversion factor rather than an estimate.

How do I convert picowatt to milliwatt?

Is the picowatt to milliwatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both picowatt and milliwatt are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e-9 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

Why do engineering unit conventions vary so much by region and industry?

Pressure, force, and stress can be expressed in metric (pascal, newton), US customary (psi, pound-force), or older and industry-specific units (atmosphere, torr, kip) depending on the country, industry, and even the equipment manufacturer, which is why engineers routinely need to convert between them when working across specs from different sources.
