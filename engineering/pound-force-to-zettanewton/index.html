Understanding the Pound-force

Pound-force (lbf) measures force. A pound-force is the force exerted by standard gravity on one pound-mass: exactly 4.4482216152605 newtons. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (engineering, aerospace)

Pound-force vs. pound-mass. Pound-force (lbf) measures force, while the plain 'pound' almost always refers to pound-mass; the two are related by standard gravity, not identical — a common source of confusion in US engineering units.

Uses of the Pound-force today

  • US engineering and aerospace force specifications
  • Torque and pressure units derived from it (psi, foot-pounds)
  • Automotive and mechanical specifications

History of the Pound-force

Pound-force was defined to let US engineers keep using the familiar pound in force calculations, fixed via standard gravity (9.80665 m/s²) acting on one pound-mass, once the pound-mass itself was standardized in 1959.

Historical origin. Pound-force was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
