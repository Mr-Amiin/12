About Converting Degrees to Milliradians

Degree and Milliradian are the two standard ways to measure angles — degrees for everyday and navigational use, radians for calculus and physics — so converting between them comes up constantly in math, engineering, and programming. Degree is most often used for navigation and cartography (latitude/longitude).

Understanding the Degree

Degree (deg) measures angle. A degree is 1/360 of a full rotation, a division dating to Babylonian base-60 astronomy. It is classified as a Non-SI unit accepted for use with SI.

Where it's used: Used worldwide for navigation, geography, and everyday angle measurement.

Uses of the Degree today

  • Navigation and cartography (latitude/longitude)
  • Construction and surveying
  • Everyday angle measurement (protractors, sports)

History of the Degree

The 360-degree circle traces to ancient Babylonian astronomy, whose base-60 (sexagesimal) number system made 360 — divisible by many small numbers — a practical choice for dividing a circle. The convention passed through Greek astronomy (Hipparchus, Ptolemy) into common use.

Historical origin. Degree was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
