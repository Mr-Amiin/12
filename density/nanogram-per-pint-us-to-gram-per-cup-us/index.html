About Converting Nanograms per Pint to Grams per Cup

Nanogram per Pint and Gram per Cup both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Both are mass per volume density units.

Formula

grams per cup = nanograms per pint × 5e-10

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 nanogram per pint equals 2.113376e-9 base units, and 1 gram per cup equals 4.22675283773 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct nanogram per pint-to-gram per cup factor of 5e-10.

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?

Density is the mass of a pure substance or material per unit volume. Mass concentration is the mass of one component — like a dissolved solute — within a mixture's total volume. The two use the same kind of units but describe different physical situations.
