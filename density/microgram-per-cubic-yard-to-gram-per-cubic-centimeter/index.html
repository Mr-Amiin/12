About Converting Micrograms per Cubic yard to Grams per Cubic Centimeter

Microgram per Cubic yard and Gram per Cubic Centimeter both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Both are mass per volume density units.

Formula

grams per cubic centimeter = micrograms per cubic yard × 1.307951e-12

Understanding the Gram per Cubic Centimeter

Gram per Cubic Centimeter (g/cm3) measures density. Numerically identical to kilograms per liter, since one cubic centimeter equals one milliliter — this is the form most commonly used in chemistry and materials-science textbooks. It is classified as a metric derived density unit.

Where it's used: Standard density unit in chemistry and materials science worldwide

Uses of the Gram per Cubic Centimeter today

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?

Density is the mass of a pure substance or material per unit volume. Mass concentration is the mass of one component — like a dissolved solute — within a mixture's total volume. The two use the same kind of units but describe different physical situations.
