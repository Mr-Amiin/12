About Converting Pounds per Gallon to Grams per Pint

Pound per Gallon and Gram per Pint both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Pound per Gallon is most often used for us paint and coatings density specifications.

Formula

grams per pint = pounds per gallon × 56.69904625

Understanding the Pound per Gallon

Pound per Gallon (lb/gal) measures density. The US customary unit for liquid density, based on the US gallon (231 cubic inches). It is classified as a US customary density unit.

Where it's used: United States (paint, fuel, and industrial liquid density specifications)

lb/gal vs. lb/ft³. Both are US customary density units; they're related through the real, fixed relationship between the US gallon and the cubic foot (1 cubic foot ≈ 7.48 US gallons), not a round conversion factor.

Uses of the Pound per Gallon today

  • US paint and coatings density specifications
  • US fuel and petroleum liquid density specs
  • Industrial liquid handling and shipping weight calculations
