About Converting Kilograms per Gallon to Centigrams per Cubic Centimeter

Kilogram per Gallon and Centigram per Cubic Centimeter both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Kilogram per Gallon is most often used for can be used to express liquid density such as fuel or industrial liquids in kilograms per gallon — in practice, kilograms per liter (metric) and pounds per gallon (us customary) are the specific pairings more commonly seen in fuel and liquid-density references.

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?
