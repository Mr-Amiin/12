About Converting Nanograms per Liter to Picograms per Cubic Millimeter

Nanogram per Liter and Picogram per Cubic Millimeter both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Both are mass per volume density units.

Formula

picograms per cubic millimeter = nanograms per liter × 0.001

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 nanogram per liter equals 1e-9 base units, and 1 picogram per cubic millimeter equals 0.000001 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct nanogram per liter-to-picogram per cubic millimeter factor of 0.001.

Simple example

1 ng/L × 0.001 = 0.001 pg/mm3

1 nanogram per liter = 0.001 picograms per cubic millimeter.

Real-world example

1,000 ng/L × 0.001 = 1 pg/mm3

1,000 nanograms per liter = 1 picograms per cubic millimeter.

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?

Density is the mass of a pure substance or material per unit volume. Mass concentration is the mass of one component — like a dissolved solute — within a mixture's total volume. The two use the same kind of units but describe different physical situations.
