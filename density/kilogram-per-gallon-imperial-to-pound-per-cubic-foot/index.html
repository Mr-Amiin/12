About Converting Kilograms per Imperial gallon to Pounds per Cubic foot

Kilogram per Imperial gallon and Pound per Cubic foot both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Kilogram per Imperial gallon is most often used for can be used to express liquid density in uk/commonwealth contexts that reference the imperial gallon — in practice, kilograms per liter is the more commonly seen metric density unit even in these regions.

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?

Density is the mass of a pure substance or material per unit volume. Mass concentration is the mass of one component — like a dissolved solute — within a mixture's total volume. The two use the same kind of units but describe different physical situations.
