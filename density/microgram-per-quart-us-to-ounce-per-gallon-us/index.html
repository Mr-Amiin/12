About Converting Micrograms per Quart to Ounces per Gallon

Microgram per Quart and Ounce per Gallon both measure density — mass per unit volume — a property used to identify materials, check manufacturing quality, and predict whether something floats or sinks. As a fixed reference point, water has a density of exactly 1000 kg/m³ (1 g/cm³, 1 g/mL) at its temperature of maximum density, which is why many density figures in science and engineering are quoted relative to water. Both are mass per volume density units.

Formula

ounces per gallon = micrograms per quart × 1.410958e-7

Understanding the Ounce per Gallon

Ounce per Gallon (oz/gal) measures concentration (dilution rate). A US customary way to express how much concentrate is mixed into each gallon of a diluted solution. It is classified as a US customary concentration unit.

Where it's used: United States

Uses of the Ounce per Gallon today

  • Used to express dilution rates on many US pesticide, herbicide, and household cleaning product labels, which commonly specify a number of fluid ounces of concentrate per gallon of water

Ounce per Gallon — sources

  • US product-label dilution conventions (pesticide/herbicide industry guides) — Fluid ounces of concentrate per gallon is a widely used label format for US spray and cleaning product dilution instructions.

What's the difference between density and mass concentration?

Density is the mass of a pure substance or material per unit volume. Mass concentration is the mass of one component — like a dissolved solute — within a mixture's total volume. The two use the same kind of units but describe different physical situations.
