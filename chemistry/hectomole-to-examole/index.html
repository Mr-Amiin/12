About Converting Hectomoles to Examoles

Hectomole and Examole both measure mass concentration — how much of a substance is dissolved or dispersed in a given volume — a core calculation for preparing lab solutions, checking water quality against a regulatory limit, or formulating a pharmaceutical dose. Specifically: Hectomole is 100 mole units, and examole is 1000000000000000000 mole units.

Formula

examoles = hectomoles × 1e-16

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 hectomole equals 100 base units, and 1 examole equals 1e+18 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct hectomole-to-examole factor of 1e-16.

What's the difference between mass concentration and molarity?

Mass concentration expresses how much mass of a substance is present per unit volume (for example, grams per liter). Molarity expresses the amount in moles per liter instead, which accounts for the substance's molecular weight. This category converts mass-concentration units.
