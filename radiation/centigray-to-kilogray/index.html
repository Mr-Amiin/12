About Converting Centigrays to Kilograys

Centigray and Kilogray both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Centigray is 0.01 gray units, and kilogray is 1000 gray units.

Formula

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 centigray equals 0.01 base units, and 1 kilogray equals 1000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct centigray-to-kilogray factor of 0.00001.

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
