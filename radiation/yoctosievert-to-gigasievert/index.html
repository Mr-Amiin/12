About Converting Yoctosieverts to Gigasieverts

Yoctosievert and Gigasievert both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Yoctosievert is 1e-24 sievert units, and gigasievert is 1000000000 sievert units.

Formula

gigasieverts = yoctosieverts × 1e-33

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 yoctosievert equals 1e-24 base units, and 1 gigasievert equals 1000000000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct yoctosievert-to-gigasievert factor of 1e-33.

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
