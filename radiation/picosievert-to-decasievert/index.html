About Converting Picosieverts to Decasieverts

Picosievert and Decasievert both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Picosievert is 1e-12 sievert units, and decasievert is 10 sievert units.

Formula

decasieverts = picosieverts × 1e-13

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 picosievert equals 1e-12 base units, and 1 decasievert equals 10 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct picosievert-to-decasievert factor of 1e-13.

Simple example

1 pSv × 1e-13 = 1e-13 daSv

1 picosievert = 1e-13 decasieverts.

Real-world example

10 pSv × 1e-13 = 1e-12 daSv

10 picosieverts = 1e-12 decasieverts.

How many decasieverts are in 1 picosievert?

Is the picosievert to decasievert conversion exact?

Yes. Both picosievert and decasievert are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e-13 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
