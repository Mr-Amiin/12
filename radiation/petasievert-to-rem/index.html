About Converting Petasieverts to Rems

Petasievert and Rem both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Petasievert is 1000000000000000 sievert units, and rem is 0.01 sievert.

Formula

rems = petasieverts × 1e+17

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petasievert equals 1e+15 base units, and 1 rem equals 0.01 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petasievert-to-rem factor of 1e+17.

Simple example

1 PSv × 1e+17 = 1e+17 rem

1 petasievert = 1e+17 rems.

Real-world example

Understanding the Rem

Rem (rem) measures equivalent radiation dose. 'Roentgen equivalent man' — the US customary unit of equivalent radiation dose, fixed at exactly 0.01 sievert. It is classified as a non-SI unit, still common in the US.

Where it's used: Still widely used in the United States alongside the SI sievert

Rem vs. sievert. Both measure equivalent radiation dose; the sievert is the modern SI unit while the rem is the older US customary unit, related by 1 sievert = 100 rem exactly.

Uses of the Rem today

  • US occupational radiation dose limits and monitoring
  • US nuclear regulatory reporting
  • US medical radiation dose records
