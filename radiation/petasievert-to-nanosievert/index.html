About Converting Petasieverts to Nanosieverts

Petasievert and Nanosievert both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Petasievert is 1000000000000000 sievert units, and nanosievert is 1e-9 sievert units.

Formula

nanosieverts = petasieverts × 1e+24

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petasievert equals 1e+15 base units, and 1 nanosievert equals 1e-9 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petasievert-to-nanosievert factor of 1e+24.

Is the petasievert to nanosievert conversion exact?

Yes. Both petasievert and nanosievert are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e+24 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
