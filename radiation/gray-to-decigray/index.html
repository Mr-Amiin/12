About Converting Grays to Decigrays

Gray and Decigray both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Gray is most often used for radiation therapy dose prescriptions in medicine.

Formula

decigrays = grays × 10

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 gray equals 1 base unit, and 1 decigray equals 0.1 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct gray-to-decigray factor of 10.

Simple example

1 Gy × 10 = 10 dGy

1 gray = 10 decigrays.

Real-world example

10 Gy × 10 = 100 dGy

10 grays = 100 decigrays.

Understanding the Gray

Gray (Gy) measures absorbed radiation dose. The SI derived unit of absorbed radiation dose, equal to one joule of radiation energy absorbed per kilogram of matter. It is classified as a SI derived unit.

Where it's used: Universal — SI derived unit used worldwide in medicine and radiation protection

Gray vs. sievert. The gray measures physically absorbed energy; the sievert measures equivalent dose, which weights the gray value for how biologically damaging a given type of radiation is. The same absorbed dose in gray can correspond to different sievert values depending on the radiation type.

Uses of the Gray today

  • Radiation therapy dose prescriptions in medicine
  • Radiation safety and occupational dose monitoring
  • Nuclear and radiological research

History of the Gray

The gray is named after Louis Harold Gray (1905–1965), a British physicist who pioneered the physics of radiation dosimetry and its biological effects, and was adopted by the CGPM as an SI derived unit in 1975.

Historical origin. Gray was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
