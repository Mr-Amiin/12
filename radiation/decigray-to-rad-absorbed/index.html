About Converting Decigrays to Rads

Decigray and Rad both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Decigray is 0.1 gray units, and rad is 0.01 gray.

Formula

rads = decigrays × 10

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 decigray equals 0.1 base units, and 1 rad equals 0.01 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct decigray-to-rad factor of 10.

Simple example

1 dGy × 10 = 10 rad

1 decigray = 10 rads.

Real-world example

10 dGy × 10 = 100 rad

10 decigrays = 100 rads.

Understanding the Rad

Rad (rad) measures absorbed radiation dose. 'Radiation absorbed dose' — the US customary unit of absorbed radiation dose, fixed at exactly 0.01 gray. It is classified as a non-SI unit, still common in the US.

Where it's used: Still used in some US medical and historical contexts alongside the SI gray

Rad vs. gray. Both measure absorbed radiation dose; the gray is the modern SI unit while the rad is the older US customary unit, related by 1 gray = 100 rad exactly.

Uses of the Rad today

  • US radiation therapy dose records (historically)
  • US nuclear regulatory and medical physics contexts
