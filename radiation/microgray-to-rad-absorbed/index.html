About Converting Micrograys to Rads

Microgray and Rad both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Microgray is 0.000001 gray units, and rad is 0.01 gray.

Formula

rads = micrograys × 0.0001

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 microgray equals 0.000001 base units, and 1 rad equals 0.01 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct microgray-to-rad factor of 0.0001.

Simple example

1 uGy × 0.0001 = 0.0001 rad

1 microgray = 0.0001 rads.

Real-world example

10 uGy × 0.0001 = 0.001 rad

Understanding the Rad

Rad (rad) measures absorbed radiation dose. 'Radiation absorbed dose' — the US customary unit of absorbed radiation dose, fixed at exactly 0.01 gray. It is classified as a non-SI unit, still common in the US.

Where it's used: Still used in some US medical and historical contexts alongside the SI gray

Rad vs. gray. Both measure absorbed radiation dose; the gray is the modern SI unit while the rad is the older US customary unit, related by 1 gray = 100 rad exactly.

Uses of the Rad today

  • US radiation therapy dose records (historically)
  • US nuclear regulatory and medical physics contexts
