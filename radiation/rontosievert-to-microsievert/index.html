About Converting Rontosieverts to Microsieverts

Rontosievert and Microsievert both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Rontosievert is 1e-27 sievert units, and microsievert is 0.000001 sievert units.

Formula

microsieverts = rontosieverts × 1e-21

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 rontosievert equals 1e-27 base units, and 1 microsievert equals 0.000001 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct rontosievert-to-microsievert factor of 1e-21.

Is the rontosievert to microsievert conversion exact?

Yes. Both rontosievert and microsievert are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e-21 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
