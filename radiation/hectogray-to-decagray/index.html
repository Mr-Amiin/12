About Converting Hectograys to Decagrays

Hectogray and Decagray both belong to the radiation-measurement family, though radiation quantities fall into genuinely distinct categories: absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures energy deposited in matter, equivalent dose (sievert, rem) weights that for biological harm, and activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays — this page only converts between units of the same kind. Specifically: Hectogray is 100 gray units, and decagray is 10 gray units.

Formula

decagrays = hectograys × 10

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 hectogray equals 100 base units, and 1 decagray equals 10 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct hectogray-to-decagray factor of 10.

Simple example

1 hGy × 10 = 10 daGy

1 hectogray = 10 decagrays.

Real-world example

What's the difference between absorbed dose, equivalent dose, and activity?

Absorbed dose (gray, rad) measures the energy radiation deposits in matter. Equivalent dose (sievert, rem) adjusts that figure for how harmful different types of radiation are to biological tissue. Activity (becquerel, curie) measures how often a radioactive source decays, independent of how much dose a person actually receives from it.
