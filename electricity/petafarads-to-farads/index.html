About Converting Petafarads to Farads

Petafarad and Farad both belong to the electricity family — voltage, current, resistance, power, or energy — units that show up constantly when working across the different standards used by household wiring (120V/240V depending on region), automotive electrical systems (12V/24V), and electronics design. Specifically: Petafarad is 1000000000000000 farad units, and farad is the SI unit of capacitance.

Formula

farads = petafarads × 1e+15

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petafarad equals 1e+15 base units, and 1 farad equals 1 base unit, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petafarad-to-farad factor of 1e+15.

Understanding the Farad

Farad (F) measures electrical capacitance. The SI derived unit of electrical capacitance. One farad is an extremely large capacitance for practical electronics — most real-world capacitors are rated in microfarads (µF) or picofarads (pF). It is classified as a SI derived unit.

Where it's used: Universal — SI derived unit used worldwide

Uses of the Farad today

  • Capacitor ratings in electronics design
  • Circuit timing and filtering calculations
  • Battery and supercapacitor energy-storage specifications

History of the Farad

The farad is named after Michael Faraday (1791–1867), an English scientist whose experiments established the laws of electromagnetic induction and electrolysis, laying much of the groundwork for practical electrical engineering.

Historical origin. Farad was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

What's the difference between voltage, current, and resistance?

Voltage (volts) is the electrical potential difference driving current through a circuit. Current (amperes) is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance (ohms) is how much a component opposes that flow. They're related by Ohm's law (V = I × R), but they are distinct physical quantities, and this page converts between units of just one of them.
