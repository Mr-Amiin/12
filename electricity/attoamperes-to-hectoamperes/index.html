About Converting Attoamperes to Hectoamperes

Attoampere and Hectoampere both belong to the electricity family — voltage, current, resistance, power, or energy — units that show up constantly when working across the different standards used by household wiring (120V/240V depending on region), automotive electrical systems (12V/24V), and electronics design. Specifically: Attoampere is 1e-18 ampere units, and hectoampere is 100 ampere units.

Formula

hectoamperes = attoamperes × 1e-20

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 attoampere equals 1e-18 base units, and 1 hectoampere equals 100 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct attoampere-to-hectoampere factor of 1e-20.

Simple example

1 aA × 1e-20 = 1e-20 hA

1 attoampere = 1e-20 hectoamperes.

Real-world example

120 aA × 1e-20 = 1.2e-18 hA

120 attoamperes = 1.2e-18 hectoamperes.

Frequently asked questions

How many hectoamperes are in 1 attoampere?

1 attoampere equals 1e-20 hectoamperes, using the exact defined conversion factor rather than an estimate.

How do I convert attoampere to hectoampere?

Multiply the attoampere value by 1e-20. The converter above does this instantly to whatever precision you set.

How do I convert hectoampere back to attoampere?

Is the attoampere to hectoampere conversion exact?

Yes. Both attoampere and hectoampere are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e-20 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between voltage, current, and resistance?

Voltage (volts) is the electrical potential difference driving current through a circuit. Current (amperes) is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance (ohms) is how much a component opposes that flow. They're related by Ohm's law (V = I × R), but they are distinct physical quantities, and this page converts between units of just one of them.
