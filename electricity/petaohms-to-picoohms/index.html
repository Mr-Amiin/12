About Converting Petaohms to Picoohms

Petaohm and Picoohm both belong to the electricity family — voltage, current, resistance, power, or energy — units that show up constantly when working across the different standards used by household wiring (120V/240V depending on region), automotive electrical systems (12V/24V), and electronics design. Specifically: Petaohm is 1000000000000000 ohm units, and picoohm is 1e-12 ohm units.

Formula

picoohms = petaohms × 1e+27

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petaohm equals 1e+15 base units, and 1 picoohm equals 1e-12 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petaohm-to-picoohm factor of 1e+27.

Simple example

1 POhm × 1e+27 = 1e+27 pOhm

1 petaohm = 1e+27 picoohms.

Real-world example

120 POhm × 1e+27 = 1.2e+29 pOhm

120 petaohms = 1.2e+29 picoohms.

Frequently asked questions

How many picoohms are in 1 petaohm?

1 petaohm equals 1e+27 picoohms, using the exact defined conversion factor rather than an estimate.

How do I convert petaohm to picoohm?

Multiply the petaohm value by 1e+27. The converter above does this instantly to whatever precision you set.

How do I convert picoohm back to petaohm?

Use the reverse factor: 1 picoohm equals 1e-27 petaohms. You can also use the swap control in the converter above to flip the direction instantly.

Is the petaohm to picoohm conversion exact?

Yes. Both petaohm and picoohm are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e+27 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between voltage, current, and resistance?

Voltage (volts) is the electrical potential difference driving current through a circuit. Current (amperes) is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance (ohms) is how much a component opposes that flow. They're related by Ohm's law (V = I × R), but they are distinct physical quantities, and this page converts between units of just one of them.
