About Converting Yoctowatts to Kilowatts

Yoctowatt and Kilowatt both belong to the electricity family — voltage, current, resistance, power, or energy — units that show up constantly when working across the different standards used by household wiring (120V/240V depending on region), automotive electrical systems (12V/24V), and electronics design. Specifically: Yoctowatt is 1e-24 watt units, and kilowatt is 1000 watt units.

Formula

kilowatts = yoctowatts × 1e-27

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 yoctowatt equals 1e-24 base units, and 1 kilowatt equals 1000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct yoctowatt-to-kilowatt factor of 1e-27.

Simple example

1 yW × 1e-27 = 1e-27 kW

1 yoctowatt = 1e-27 kilowatts.

Real-world example

120 yW × 1e-27 = 1.2e-25 kW

120 yoctowatts = 1.2e-25 kilowatts.

Understanding the Kilowatt

Kilowatt (kW) measures power. A kilowatt is 1,000 watts. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard power unit worldwide for larger appliances, vehicles, and electrical systems

Uses of the Kilowatt today

  • Electric vehicle motor power ratings (alongside horsepower)
  • Household electrical system and generator capacity
  • Industrial motor and equipment power ratings

History of the Kilowatt

The kilowatt follows directly from the watt via the standard SI 'kilo-' prefix, becoming especially prominent with the growth of electrical power generation and distribution in the late 19th and 20th centuries.

Historical origin. Kilowatt was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

How many kilowatts are in 1 yoctowatt?

What is a kilowatt?

Is the yoctowatt to kilowatt conversion exact?

Yes. Both yoctowatt and kilowatt are defined by fixed standards rather than physical artifacts, so the factor of 1e-27 used above is exact to as many digits as you choose to display.

What's the difference between voltage, current, and resistance?

Voltage (volts) is the electrical potential difference driving current through a circuit. Current (amperes) is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance (ohms) is how much a component opposes that flow. They're related by Ohm's law (V = I × R), but they are distinct physical quantities, and this page converts between units of just one of them.
