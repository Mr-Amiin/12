About Converting Kilovolts to Gigavolts

Kilovolt and Gigavolt both belong to the electricity family — voltage, current, resistance, power, or energy — units that show up constantly when working across the different standards used by household wiring (120V/240V depending on region), automotive electrical systems (12V/24V), and electronics design. Specifically: Kilovolt is 1000 volt units, and gigavolt is 1000000000 volt units.

Formula

gigavolts = kilovolts × 0.000001

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 kilovolt equals 1000 base units, and 1 gigavolt equals 1000000000 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct kilovolt-to-gigavolt factor of 0.000001.

Simple example

1 kV × 0.000001 = 0.000001 GV

1 kilovolt = 0.000001 gigavolts.

Real-world example

120 kV × 0.000001 = 0.00012 GV

120 kilovolts = 0.00012 gigavolts.

Is the kilovolt to gigavolt conversion exact?

What's the difference between voltage, current, and resistance?

Voltage (volts) is the electrical potential difference driving current through a circuit. Current (amperes) is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance (ohms) is how much a component opposes that flow. They're related by Ohm's law (V = I × R), but they are distinct physical quantities, and this page converts between units of just one of them.
