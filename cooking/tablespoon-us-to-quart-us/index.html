About Converting Tablespoons to Quarts

Tablespoon and Quart both show up in recipes, and converting between them matters whenever a recipe from one country's cookbook needs to be followed with another country's measuring tools. Tablespoon is typically associated with united States (cooking and recipe measurement), while Quart is typically associated with united States (cooking, dairy, motor oil packaging).

Formula

quarts = tablespoons × 0.015625

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 tablespoon equals 0.0000147867647813 base units, and 1 quart equals 0.000946352946 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct tablespoon-to-quart factor of 0.015625.

Understanding the Tablespoon

Tablespoon (tbsp) measures volume. The US tablespoon is exactly 14.78676478125 milliliters — 3 US teaspoons, or 1/16 of a US cup. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (cooking and recipe measurement)

Uses of the Tablespoon today

  • US recipe and cooking measurement
  • Nutrition labeling serving sizes in the US
  • Medication dosing instructions in the US

Understanding the Quart

Quart (qt) measures volume. The US liquid quart is exactly one-quarter of a US gallon: 0.946352946 liters. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (cooking, dairy, motor oil packaging)

Uses of the Quart today

  • US recipe measurement (larger liquid volumes)
  • US motor oil and automotive fluid packaging
  • US dairy and beverage packaging (quart containers)

History of the Quart

The quart ('quarter' of a gallon) follows the same English wine-gallon lineage as the US gallon, retained by the US after England moved to a different imperial standard in 1824.

Historical origin. Quart was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
