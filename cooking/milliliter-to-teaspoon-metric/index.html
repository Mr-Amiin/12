About Converting Milliliters to Metric teaspoons

Milliliter and Metric teaspoon both show up in recipes, and converting between them matters whenever a recipe from one country's cookbook needs to be followed with another country's measuring tools. Milliliter is most often used for medicine dosing (worldwide, including in the us).

Formula

metric teaspoons = milliliters × 0.2

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 milliliter equals 0.000001 base units, and 1 metric teaspoon equals 0.000005 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct milliliter-to-metric teaspoon factor of 0.2.

Simple example

1 mL × 0.2 = 0.2 metric tsp

1 milliliter = 0.2 metric teaspoons.

Real-world example

2 mL × 0.2 = 0.4 metric tsp

2 milliliters = 0.4 metric teaspoons.

Understanding the Milliliter

Milliliter (mL) measures volume. A milliliter is one-thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small-volume unit worldwide

Uses of the Milliliter today

  • Medicine dosing (worldwide, including in the US)
  • Cooking and recipe measurement outside the US
  • Beverage and small-container labeling

History of the Milliliter

The milliliter follows directly from the liter via the 'milli-' SI prefix. It is notable as one of the few metric units in routine US use, since medicine dosing (syringes, liquid medication) is specified in milliliters even in the US.

Historical origin. Milliliter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Metric teaspoon (metric tsp)

A metric teaspoon is 5 milliliters.
