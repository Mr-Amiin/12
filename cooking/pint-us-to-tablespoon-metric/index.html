About Converting Pints to Metric tablespoons

Pint and Metric tablespoon both show up in recipes, and converting between them matters whenever a recipe from one country's cookbook needs to be followed with another country's measuring tools. Pint is most often used for us beer and beverage serving sizes.

Understanding the Pint

Pint (pt) measures volume. The US liquid pint is one-eighth of a US gallon: exactly 0.473176473 liters. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (beer serving, cooking, dairy)

Uses of the Pint today

  • US beer and beverage serving sizes
  • US recipe measurement
  • US dairy packaging (pint containers)

History of the Pint

The pint follows the same English wine-gallon lineage as the US gallon and quart, retained by the US after Britain's 1824 switch to a different imperial gallon standard.

Historical origin. Pint was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Metric tablespoon (metric tbsp)

A metric tablespoon is 15 milliliters.
