About Converting Miles per imperial gallon to Liters per kilometer

Miles per imperial gallon and Liters per kilometer are two of the ways the world measures fuel efficiency, and converting between them is essential for comparing a US car's window-sticker rating to a European or Asian one. Miles per imperial gallon is most often used for uk vehicle fuel-economy figures (particularly older or enthusiast/import contexts).

Understanding the Miles per imperial gallon

Miles per imperial gallon (mpg imp) measures fuel economy. Miles per imperial gallon measures distance per imperial gallon (4.54609 L) — about 20% larger than the US gallon, so an imperial MPG figure is correspondingly higher for the same real efficiency. It is classified as a UK/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United Kingdom (historically; UK now also uses L/100km)

US mpg vs. imperial mpg. The identical term 'mpg' means a different amount depending on which gallon is used — an imperial mpg figure is about 20% higher than the equivalent US mpg for the same actual fuel efficiency, a common source of confusion when comparing US and UK/Canadian car specs.

Uses of the Miles per imperial gallon today

  • UK vehicle fuel-economy figures (particularly older or enthusiast/import contexts)
  • Comparing UK-market vehicle specifications to US ones

History of the Miles per imperial gallon

Imperial MPG follows the same automotive tradition as US MPG but uses Britain's imperial gallon (standardized in 1824), leading to the two systems diverging even though they share identical terminology.

Historical origin. Miles per imperial gallon was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
