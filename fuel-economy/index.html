About Fuel Economy Conversion

Fuel economy units express how far a vehicle travels per unit of fuel (miles per gallon, kilometers per liter) or how much fuel it consumes per unit of distance (liters per 100 kilometers). These are reciprocal relationships, not a simple ratio: converting between a distance-per-volume unit and a volume-per-distance unit requires inverting the value, which this converter and its underlying math handle directly. Common uses include comparing vehicles across US and international fuel-economy conventions, and estimating fuel costs for a given distance.

Quick Facts

  • US standard: miles per gallon (mpg)
  • International standard: liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km) and kilometers per liter (km/L)
  • Higher mpg means better efficiency; lower L/100 km means better efficiency - the two scales run in opposite directions
  • Common uses
    • Vehicle shopping and fuel-cost estimation
    • Comparing US-market and international-market vehicle specifications
    • Trip fuel-cost planning

Conversion Formulas

  • Miles per US gallon to Liters per 100 km: divide 235.215 by the mpg value
  • Liters per 100 km to Miles per US gallon: divide 235.215 by the L/100km value
  • Miles per US gallon to Kilometers per liter: multiply by 0.425144
  • Kilometers per liter to Miles per US gallon: multiply by 2.35215
  • These are reciprocal relationships for the distance-per-volume vs. volume-per-distance pairs, not simple multiplication - the converter above applies the correct direction automatically
