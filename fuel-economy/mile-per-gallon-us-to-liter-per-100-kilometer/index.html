About Converting Miles per US gallon to Liters per 100 kilometers

Miles per US gallon and Liters per 100 kilometers are two of the ways the world measures fuel efficiency, and converting between them is essential for comparing a US car's window-sticker rating to a European or Asian one. Miles per US gallon is typically associated with united States (vehicle fuel-economy ratings and labeling), while Liters per 100 kilometers is typically associated with standard fuel-economy unit across most of Europe, Canada, Australia, and elsewhere.

Understanding the Miles per US gallon

Miles per US gallon (mpg) measures fuel economy. Miles per US gallon measures distance traveled per US gallon of fuel consumed — higher values mean better efficiency. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (vehicle fuel-economy ratings and labeling)

Uses of the Miles per US gallon today

  • US EPA fuel economy window stickers on new vehicles
  • Everyday US driver fuel-cost estimation

History of the Miles per US gallon

MPG became the standard US fuel-economy metric alongside the rise of mass automobile ownership in the 20th century, with the EPA establishing standardized testing and labeling requirements starting in the 1970s following the 1973 oil crisis.

Historical origin. Miles per US gallon was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Liters per 100 kilometers

Liters per 100 kilometers (L/100 km) measures fuel consumption. Liters per 100 kilometers measures fuel consumed to travel 100 km — lower values mean better efficiency, the reverse convention from mpg/km-per-L. It is classified as a Metric unit.

Where it's used: Standard fuel-economy unit across most of Europe, Canada, Australia, and elsewhere

Uses of the Liters per 100 kilometers today

  • Official vehicle fuel-consumption labeling across most of the world outside the US
  • European and Canadian vehicle efficiency standards and taxation

History of the Liters per 100 kilometers

L/100km inverts the 'efficiency' framing of mpg/km-per-L into a 'consumption' framing (how much fuel is used for a fixed distance), which many transport agencies consider more intuitive for comparing costs and emissions — it became the standard EU fuel-labeling metric as part of vehicle emissions and efficiency regulations from the late 20th century onward.

Historical origin. Liters per 100 kilometers was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
