Understanding the Kilometers per liter

Kilometers per liter (km/L) measures fuel economy. Kilometers per liter measures distance traveled per liter of fuel — higher values mean better efficiency, the metric-world counterpart to mpg. It is classified as a Metric unit.

Where it's used: Used in much of Asia and parts of Europe and Latin America for fuel-economy reporting

Uses of the Kilometers per liter today

  • Vehicle fuel-economy specifications outside the US and much of Europe
  • Motorcycle and small-vehicle efficiency ratings (common in Asian markets)

History of the Kilometers per liter

Km/L is a straightforward metric analog to mpg, following naturally once countries adopted the metric system for both distance and volume; it remains common in markets (e.g. Japan, India, Southeast Asia) where consumers are used to thinking in an 'efficiency' (distance-per-volume) framing rather than 'consumption' (volume-per-distance).

Historical origin. Kilometers per liter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
