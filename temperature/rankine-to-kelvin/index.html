Understanding the Kelvin

Kelvin (K) measures temperature. The kelvin is the SI base unit of thermodynamic temperature; 0 K (absolute zero) is the theoretical absence of thermal energy. It is classified as a SI base unit.

Where it's used: Standard scientific temperature unit worldwide

Uses of the Kelvin today

  • Scientific and engineering temperature measurement (physics, chemistry)
  • Color temperature specifications (lighting, displays)
  • Cryogenics and low-temperature physics

History of the Kelvin

The kelvin scale was proposed by William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) in 1848, building on Sadi Carnot's thermodynamic work to define an absolute temperature scale independent of any particular substance's properties, with zero set at absolute zero.

Since 2019, the kelvin has been defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant at an exact value, tying it to a fundamental physical constant rather than the triple point of water used previously.

Historical origin. Kelvin was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Is the Rankine to Kelvin conversion exact?
