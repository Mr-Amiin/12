Understanding the Metric horsepower

Metric horsepower (PS) measures power. Metric horsepower (PS/CV) is defined as exactly 735.49875 watts, the power to raise 75 kilograms one meter per second. It is classified as a Non-SI unit (metric, automotive).

Where it's used: Continental Europe, Japan, and other metric-system automotive markets

Uses of the Metric horsepower today

  • European and Japanese vehicle engine power ratings (often shown as PS or CV)
  • Motor and machinery power specifications in metric countries

History of the Metric horsepower

Metric horsepower emerged in continental Europe in the 19th century as engineers sought a metric-system analog to Watt's English horsepower, defining it via a round figure (75 kgf·m/s) convenient in metric calculations. It remains the standard automotive power unit across much of Europe and Asia, labeled PS (German 'Pferdestärke') or CV (French/Italian 'cheval-vapeur').

Historical origin. Metric horsepower was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
