About Converting Horsepowers to Zeptowatts

Horsepower and Zeptowatt both describe power output, and the conversion is a routine part of comparing an engine or motor's spec sheet across countries that use different power units. Horsepower is most often used for us and uk vehicle engine power ratings.

Formula

zeptowatts = horsepowers × 7.456999e+23

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 horsepower equals 745.699871582 base units, and 1 zeptowatt equals 1e-21 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct horsepower-to-zeptowatt factor of 7.456999e+23.

Understanding the Horsepower

Horsepower (hp) measures power. Mechanical horsepower is defined as exactly 745.6998715822702 watts. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States and United Kingdom (vehicle and engine specifications)

Mechanical horsepower vs. metric horsepower. Mechanical horsepower (745.7 W) and metric horsepower/PS (735.5 W) differ by about 1.4% — a US car spec sheet in 'hp' and a European one in 'PS' are not directly comparable numbers without conversion.

Uses of the Horsepower today

  • US and UK vehicle engine power ratings
  • Motor and engine specifications in English-speaking markets

History of the Horsepower

James Watt popularized horsepower in the 1780s as a marketing figure, comparing his steam engines' output to the working capacity of a horse (based on his estimate of a horse turning a mill), so buyers could grasp engine capability in familiar terms. The unit stuck long after its origin as a sales tool, eventually being formally defined at exactly 550 foot-pounds per second.

Historical origin. Horsepower was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
