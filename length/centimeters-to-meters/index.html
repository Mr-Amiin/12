About Converting Centimeters to Meters

Centimeter and Meter both measure length. Centimeter is typically associated with standard small-length unit worldwide except the US, while Meter is typically associated with standard length unit worldwide except the US.

Formula

meters = centimeters × 0.01

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 centimeter equals 0.01 base units, and 1 meter equals 1 base unit, so dividing one by the other gives the direct centimeter-to-meter factor of 0.01.

Simple example

1 cm × 0.01 = 0.01 m

1 centimeter = 0.01 meters.

Real-world example

10 cm × 0.01 = 0.1 m

10 centimeters = 0.1 meters.

Understanding the Centimeter

Centimeter (cm) measures distance. A centimeter is one-hundredth of a meter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small-length unit worldwide except the US

Uses of the Centimeter today

  • Everyday small-object and body measurement (height, clothing sizes) outside the US
  • Construction and manufacturing tolerances internationally

History of the Centimeter

The centimeter follows directly from the meter via the 'centi-' SI prefix (10⁻²), introduced with the metric system in the 1790s.

Historical origin. Centimeter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Meter

Meter (m) measures distance. The meter is the SI base unit of length, currently defined via the fixed speed of light. It is classified as a SI base unit.

Where it's used: Standard length unit worldwide except the US

Uses of the Meter today

  • Universal scientific and everyday length measurement outside the US
  • Construction, engineering, and manufacturing internationally

History of the Meter

Defined during the French Revolutionary metric reforms as one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the Equator, the meter has been redefined several times for precision — a platinum-iridium bar (1889), a krypton-86 wavelength (1960), and since 1983, the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458 of a second.

Historical origin. Meter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
