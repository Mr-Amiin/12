Understanding the Millimeter

Millimeter (mm) measures distance. A millimeter is one-thousandth of a meter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small/precision length unit worldwide, including in US engineering and manufacturing

Uses of the Millimeter today

  • Engineering drawings and manufacturing tolerances worldwide (including in the US)
  • Rainfall measurement in most countries
  • Small-scale everyday measurement (screen sizes, hardware)

History of the Millimeter

The millimeter follows from the meter via the 'milli-' SI prefix (10⁻³). It is notable as one of the few metric units in routine US technical/engineering use, since precision manufacturing and electronics are often specified in millimeters even in the US.

Historical origin. Millimeter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Foot

Foot (ft) measures distance. The international foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meters. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (height, construction, aviation altitude) United Kingdom (height, some construction contexts)

Uses of the Foot today

  • Height measurement in the US and UK
  • US construction and real estate (square footage)
  • Aviation altitude worldwide (feet remain standard even in metric countries)

History of the Foot

The foot has ancient roots (Roman 'pes', various regional English feet), standardized into the modern English foot over centuries. The international foot (exactly 0.3048 m) was fixed by the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement, resolving small historical differences between US and UK measurements.

Historical origin. Foot was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
