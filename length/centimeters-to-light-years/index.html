Understanding the Centimeter

Centimeter (cm) measures distance. A centimeter is one-hundredth of a meter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small-length unit worldwide except the US

Uses of the Centimeter today

  • Everyday small-object and body measurement (height, clothing sizes) outside the US
  • Construction and manufacturing tolerances internationally

History of the Centimeter

The centimeter follows directly from the meter via the 'centi-' SI prefix (10⁻²), introduced with the metric system in the 1790s.

Historical origin. Centimeter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Light-year

Light-year (ly) measures distance. A light-year is the distance light travels in one Julian year (365.25 days) in vacuum: exactly 9,460,730,472,580,800 meters. It is classified as a Non-SI unit (astronomical).

Where it's used: Used internationally in astronomy and popular science for interstellar distances.

Uses of the Light-year today

  • Measuring distances to stars and galaxies
  • Popular science communication about the scale of the universe
  • Astrophysics research

History of the Light-year

The light-year emerged in 19th-century astronomy as scientists began measuring stellar distances via parallax (Friedrich Bessel's 1838 measurement of 61 Cygni is often cited as the first reliable stellar distance). Expressing these vast distances in light-travel-time became a practical convention, and the unit's value follows directly from the defined speed of light and the length of a Julian year.

Historical origin. Light-year was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
