About Converting Inches to Millimeters

Inch and Millimeter both measure length. Inch is typically associated with united States and United Kingdom (small measurements, screen sizes, hardware), while Millimeter is typically associated with standard small/precision length unit worldwide, including in US engineering and manufacturing.

Formula

millimeters = inches × 25.4

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 inch equals 0.0254 base units, and 1 millimeter equals 0.001 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct inch-to-millimeter factor of 25.4.

Simple example

1 in × 25.4 = 25.4 mm

1 inch = 25.4 millimeters.

Real-world example

10 in × 25.4 = 254 mm

10 inches = 254 millimeters.

Understanding the Inch

Inch (in) measures distance. The international inch is defined as exactly 25.4 millimeters. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States and United Kingdom (small measurements, screen sizes, hardware)

Uses of the Inch today

  • Screen and display size measurement worldwide (even in metric countries)
  • US/UK small-object and body measurement
  • Hardware, tools, and pipe fittings (US)

History of the Inch

The inch traces to medieval England, traditionally defined as the length of three barleycorns, later standardized as 1/12 of a foot. The international inch (exactly 25.4 mm) was fixed by the same 1959 agreement that standardized the foot and yard.

Historical origin. Inch was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Millimeter

Millimeter (mm) measures distance. A millimeter is one-thousandth of a meter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small/precision length unit worldwide, including in US engineering and manufacturing

Uses of the Millimeter today

  • Engineering drawings and manufacturing tolerances worldwide (including in the US)
  • Rainfall measurement in most countries
  • Small-scale everyday measurement (screen sizes, hardware)

History of the Millimeter

The millimeter follows from the meter via the 'milli-' SI prefix (10⁻³). It is notable as one of the few metric units in routine US technical/engineering use, since precision manufacturing and electronics are often specified in millimeters even in the US.

Historical origin. Millimeter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
