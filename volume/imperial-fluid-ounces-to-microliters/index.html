About Converting Imperial fluid ounces to Microliters

Imperial fluid ounce and Microliter appear side by side at fuel pumps and on beverage labels, since the US measures liquids in gallons while most of the world uses liters. Imperial fluid ounce is most often used for uk/commonwealth beverage measurement in historical or specialized contexts.

Understanding the Imperial fluid ounce

Imperial fluid ounce (imp fl oz) measures volume. The imperial fluid ounce is 1/160 of an imperial gallon: exactly 28.4130625 milliliters — slightly smaller than the US fluid ounce despite the larger imperial gallon, because it divides that gallon into more parts. It is classified as a UK/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United Kingdom (historically; less common in current UK retail than metric units)

Imperial fluid ounce vs. US fluid ounce. Confusingly, the imperial fluid ounce (28.41 mL) is slightly smaller than the US fluid ounce (29.57 mL), even though the imperial gallon is larger overall — because the imperial gallon is divided into 160 fluid ounces rather than the US gallon's 128.

Uses of the Imperial fluid ounce today

  • UK/Commonwealth beverage measurement in historical or specialized contexts

History of the Imperial fluid ounce

The imperial fluid ounce follows from Britain's 1824 imperial gallon, divided into 160 parts rather than the 128 used in the US system, producing its counterintuitively smaller size relative to the US fluid ounce.

Historical origin. Imperial fluid ounce was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
