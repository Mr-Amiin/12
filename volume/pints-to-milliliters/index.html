About Converting Pints to Milliliters

Pint and Milliliter appear side by side at fuel pumps and on beverage labels, since the US measures liquids in gallons while most of the world uses liters. Pint is typically associated with united States (beer serving, cooking, dairy), while Milliliter is typically associated with standard small-volume unit worldwide.

Understanding the Pint

Pint (pt) measures volume. The US liquid pint is one-eighth of a US gallon: exactly 0.473176473 liters. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (beer serving, cooking, dairy)

Uses of the Pint today

  • US beer and beverage serving sizes
  • US recipe measurement
  • US dairy packaging (pint containers)

History of the Pint

The pint follows the same English wine-gallon lineage as the US gallon and quart, retained by the US after Britain's 1824 switch to a different imperial gallon standard.

Historical origin. Pint was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Milliliter

Milliliter (mL) measures volume. A milliliter is one-thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small-volume unit worldwide

Uses of the Milliliter today

  • Medicine dosing (worldwide, including in the US)
  • Cooking and recipe measurement outside the US
  • Beverage and small-container labeling

History of the Milliliter

The milliliter follows directly from the liter via the 'milli-' SI prefix. It is notable as one of the few metric units in routine US use, since medicine dosing (syringes, liquid medication) is specified in milliliters even in the US.

Historical origin. Milliliter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
