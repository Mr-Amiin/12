Understanding the Tablespoon

Tablespoon (tbsp) measures volume. The US tablespoon is exactly 14.78676478125 milliliters — 3 US teaspoons, or 1/16 of a US cup. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (cooking and recipe measurement)

Uses of the Tablespoon today

  • US recipe and cooking measurement
  • Nutrition labeling serving sizes in the US
  • Medication dosing instructions in the US

Understanding the Cubic foot

Cubic foot (ft3) measures volume. A cubic foot is exactly 0.028316846592 cubic meters, derived from the 1959 international foot. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (construction, HVAC, natural gas billing)

Uses of the Cubic foot today

  • US natural gas utility billing (often in hundreds or thousands of cubic feet)
  • HVAC airflow and building-volume calculations
  • Lumber, concrete, and construction material estimation in the US

History of the Cubic foot

The cubic foot, like the square foot, follows from the standardized foot (exactly 0.3048 m since the 1959 international agreement) rather than having a separate historical definition.

Historical origin. Cubic foot was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
