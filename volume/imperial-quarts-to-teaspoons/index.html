Understanding the Imperial quart

Imperial quart (imp qt) measures volume. The imperial quart is one-quarter of an imperial gallon: exactly 1.1365225 liters. It is classified as a UK/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United Kingdom and Commonwealth countries (historically)

Imperial quart vs. US quart. Like the gallon and pint, the imperial quart is about 20% larger than the US quart, following from Britain's 1824 gallon redefinition.

Uses of the Imperial quart today

  • UK/Commonwealth liquid measurement in historical or specialized contexts

History of the Imperial quart

The imperial quart follows directly from the 1824 imperial gallon as one-quarter of it, part of the same UK standardization that diverged from the US gallon system.

Historical origin. Imperial quart was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Teaspoon

Teaspoon (tsp) measures volume. The US teaspoon is exactly 4.92892159375 milliliters — 1/3 of a US tablespoon and 1/48 of a US cup. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (cooking and recipe measurement)

Uses of the Teaspoon today

  • US recipe and cooking measurement for small quantities
  • Medication dosing instructions in the US
  • Baking measurement alongside the tablespoon and cup
