About Converting Petaliters to Milliliters

Petaliter and Milliliter appear side by side at fuel pumps and on beverage labels, since the US measures liquids in gallons while most of the world uses liters. Specifically: Petaliter is 1000000000000000 liter units, and milliliter is 0.001 liter units.

Formula

milliliters = petaliters × 1e+18

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 petaliter equals 1e+12 base units, and 1 milliliter equals 0.000001 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct petaliter-to-milliliter factor of 1e+18.

1 PL × 1e+18 = 1e+18 mL

1 petaliter = 1e+18 milliliters.

Understanding the Milliliter

Milliliter (mL) measures volume. A milliliter is one-thousandth of a liter, equal to one cubic centimeter. It is classified as a SI derived (metric prefix) unit.

Where it's used: Standard small-volume unit worldwide

Uses of the Milliliter today

  • Medicine dosing (worldwide, including in the US)
  • Cooking and recipe measurement outside the US
  • Beverage and small-container labeling

History of the Milliliter

The milliliter follows directly from the liter via the 'milli-' SI prefix. It is notable as one of the few metric units in routine US use, since medicine dosing (syringes, liquid medication) is specified in milliliters even in the US.

Historical origin. Milliliter was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
