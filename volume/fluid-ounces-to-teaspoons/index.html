About Converting Fluid ounces to Teaspoons

Fluid ounce and Teaspoon appear side by side at fuel pumps and on beverage labels, since the US measures liquids in gallons while most of the world uses liters. Fluid ounce is typically associated with united States (beverage, nutrition labeling), while Teaspoon is typically associated with united States (cooking and recipe measurement).

Formula

teaspoons = fluid ounces × 6

This factor comes from each unit's defined relationship to the category's base unit: 1 fluid ounce equals 0.0000295735295625 base units, and 1 teaspoon equals 0.00000492892159375 base units, so dividing one by the other gives the direct fluid ounce-to-teaspoon factor of 6.

Understanding the Fluid ounce

Fluid ounce (fl oz) measures volume. The US fluid ounce is 1/128 of a US gallon: exactly 29.5735295625 milliliters. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (beverage, nutrition labeling)

Uses of the Fluid ounce today

  • US beverage container sizing (soda, water bottles)
  • US nutrition and medication dosing labels

History of the Fluid ounce

The US fluid ounce follows from the same English wine-gallon system as the rest of the US liquid volume units, defined as a consistent fraction of the gallon.

Historical origin. Fluid ounce was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Understanding the Teaspoon

Teaspoon (tsp) measures volume. The US teaspoon is exactly 4.92892159375 milliliters — 1/3 of a US tablespoon and 1/48 of a US cup. It is classified as a US customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (cooking and recipe measurement)

Uses of the Teaspoon today

  • US recipe and cooking measurement for small quantities
  • Medication dosing instructions in the US
  • Baking measurement alongside the tablespoon and cup
