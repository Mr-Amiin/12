Understanding the Imperial gallon

Imperial gallon (imp gal) measures volume. The imperial gallon is defined as exactly 4.54609 liters, about 20% larger than the US gallon. It is classified as a UK/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United Kingdom, Canada (some contexts), and other Commonwealth countries

Imperial gallon vs. US gallon. Britain's 1824 Weights and Measures Act defined a new gallon based on 10 pounds of water, replacing the old wine gallon the US had already adopted — leaving the US (3.785 L) and imperial (4.546 L) gallons about 20% apart today.

Uses of the Imperial gallon today

  • UK fuel and beverage measurement (historically; UK now primarily uses liters for retail)
  • Commonwealth country liquid measurement in some contexts

History of the Imperial gallon

The imperial gallon was created by Britain's Weights and Measures Act of 1824, defined as the volume of 10 pounds of water, replacing several inconsistent older English gallon definitions (wine gallon, ale gallon, corn gallon) with one standard.

Historical origin. Imperial gallon was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.

Imperial gallon — sources

  • UK Weights and Measures Act 1824 — Establishes the imperial gallon definition.
