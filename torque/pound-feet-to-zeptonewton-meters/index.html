Understanding the Pound-foot

Pound-foot (lb ft) measures torque. A pound-foot is the torque from one pound-force applied at one foot of leverage: exactly 1.3558179483314004 newton-meters. It is classified as a US/imperial customary unit.

Where it's used: United States (automotive and mechanical torque specifications)

Pound-foot vs. foot-pound. Pound-foot (torque) and foot-pound (energy/work) use the same two units but measure physically different quantities; US convention typically orders them differently to help distinguish which is meant, though usage isn't always consistent.

Uses of the Pound-foot today

  • US torque wrench specifications (bolt tightening)
  • US automotive engine torque specifications
  • US mechanical and construction trades

History of the Pound-foot

The pound-foot follows from pound-force and the foot, both standardized through English and later international agreements, remaining the standard US automotive and mechanical torque unit even as most other measurements shifted toward metric in engineering contexts.

Historical origin. Pound-foot was not the invention of a single named person; it developed through the process described above.
